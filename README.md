# Farm — build 1

A browser farming game: Stardew on the surface, Cookie Clicker underneath, crops bred from genes. See `PLAN.md` for the design.

## Run
```
npm start          # node server.js → http://localhost:8080  (any static server works too)
npm test           # rule tests in Node, no browser: node tests/run.js
node tests/render-smoke.js   # draws 200+ genomes through every stage with a stub canvas
```
No build step, no dependencies. Vanilla ES modules + Canvas 2D. Saves to localStorage (export/import under `P`).

## Controls
WASD / arrows move; number keys 1–5, scrolling or clicking pick a hotbar slot (5 slots); E opens the station you stand
by (seed rack, crate, sign) or closes a panel, silently does nothing elsewhere; Esc closes a panel; everything else is
left click. Click a rack while holding its tool to hang it back up (the can keeps its water). Hold left click on a movable
object (tool racks, seed rack) for half a second to lift it: you carry it above your head at half speed, and a click
on open grass in reach puts it down. The seed rack panel (E) shows the tray, drying progress, what it will give, and
your produce with a "Dry one" button per crop.
Two racks in front of the house hold the hoe and the watering can: click one to take its tool. Empty hands on a ripe crop
harvests it. Empty hands on the well drinks (thirst). Click your own character while holding produce to eat it (hunger);
sleeping does not refill either. Click the house door after 6pm to sleep. The house's porch row is walkable; only the
walls are solid. Click grass in reach with the hoe: a three-swing
animation with a progress bar, then tilled soil. Click the well holding the can to draw water (8 pours). Click tilled soil
with the can to pour. Hover anything for the layered card (tile, soil moisture, what's in the soil, plant, health, time
to ripe). Once there is bare tilled soil a bird comes by ONCE (the starter event) and drops a wild seed on it; it stays
unknown until it ripens. Harvesting never gives seeds: lay one produce on the seed rack and it dries into seeds. Seeds
are fruits-per-harvest × 2 rolled within ±35 % (`seedRack.seedVariance`), never under 2, counted when the tray is done;
sterile species give none. Drying takes 4 game hours scaled by the fruit: big and juicy (size, thirst genes) dries
slowly, small dry grain fast. Hover the rack or press E for progress, click it when the seeds are ready.
Soil moisture is 0–100 %: plants grow from 20 %, fastest at 40–85 %, suffer drought below 15 % and waterlogging above 90 %.

`lab.html` is the breeding lab: a standalone page that runs the real `cross()` and crop renderer. Pick two parents,
breed with a seed, and read every gene decision (from A / from B / blend / mutated), the rare-trait rolls, the

Shop, breeder and compost are still in `src/sim.js` and tested; they return as each piece is designed.

Scene: a near-fullscreen canvas at 64 px per tile (`scale` in config), a 64×48 map that is all forest except the clearing:
the house and the 2×2 chunks of 4×4 tiles (8×8) you own. Buying a chunk clears its trees. HUD is drawn on the canvas in a
wooden pixel style with the open-licensed Press Start 2P font (`assets/fonts`): money, day and time with a sun/moon/rain
icon, health / hunger / thirst bars (hunger and thirst drain over 36 h and 24 h; at zero they drain energy; sleep refills
them until food exists), and an empty hotbar.

Hoe tills (hoe again flattens; on a ripe crop it harvests; on a withered one it clears). Watering can wets tilled soil for
10 game hours. Seeds plant on tilled soil. Any tool on a ripe crop harvests it. Fertiliser goes on tilled soil before
planting. Sprinklers are placed on free farmland and water their 3×3 each morning.

## What is in
- Stage 1–3: grid farm, energy, day clock (8 real minutes per day, forced sleep at 2am), growth only while wet, drying →
  health loss → withering, ripe window → rot → death, rain, land chunks, can upgrades (1 → 3 → 9 tiles), chest + chest
  upgrades, energy upgrades, sprinklers, instant sell at the stall, seed drops on harvest, regrowing crops, neighbour bonus.
- Stage 4: the Breeder. Genome of 26 genes + 5 recessive rare traits, blend/pick inheritance, mutation, instability →
  success / sterile / wither / revert, generated names, rarity tiers, price from genes with no cap (coins per minute =
  rateBase × rateGrowth^rarity, and rarity keeps rising with generations and traits), seed book with a +2 % sale bonus.
- Compost bin → six-stat fertilisers with a power budget derived from what you put in, fertiliser book.
- Save/load with offline progress (capped at one game day), export/import.
- Code-drawn pixel art: 7 shop species (3 at the start, 4 unlocked by land) and every bred species drawn from genes
  across 4 stages + withered.

## Where to tweak
- `src/config.js` — every number: day length, soil wet time, death/rot rates, energy costs, prices and cost growth,
  tier thresholds and coins-per-minute per tier, mutation and instability, seed drops, compost budget.
- `src/genetics.js` — gene table and rarity weights (`GENES`), starter genomes (`STARTER_GENOMES`), naming tables
  (`NOUNS`, colour words), `produceValue`.
- `src/render/crops.js` — how genes become pixels: `drawFoliage` per family, `drawProduce` per archetype, `PLACES`.
- `src/render/sprites.js`, `src/render/palette.js` — tiles, buildings, player, palette.
- `src/world.js` — map layout, building positions, chunk range.
- `src/sim.js` — the rules (tick, actions, sleep, shop, breeder, compost). `src/ui.js` — panels. `src/game.js` — loop/render.

## Likely first tweaks
Breeding currently succeeds ~80 % of the time with starters and discoveries come fast; raise `genetics.mutationInstability`
or `rarityInstability` to slow it. Land cost growth (`economy.landGrowth`) and tier rates (`tiers.ratePerMinute`) set the
pace of the whole economy.

**Build 26.** The compost bin. It is a catalogue item (Stations, $60) delivered as a parcel you carry and put down like the bench. Click it holding a crop or a withered plant to drop one in (0.35 s each, held or not, Quick hands speeds it); it takes 12. The lid shuts when you sleep and a heap of 3 or more has rotted down by morning into bags of fertiliser, one per three plants, waiting in the bin with an arrow over it. Take them with empty hands; click a tilled bed with a bag in hand to work it in, and the bed shows dark crumbs until that plant is pulled. What goes in sets what comes out: fast crops give Speed, many-fruited give Yield, hardy give Hardiness, low-thirst give Water keep, rarer crops give Quality (stars on the harvest, each +30% on the price, starred crops stack apart), bred give Mutation. E opens the bin: the heap on the left (click a plant to take it back), what it will make on the right, your produce below, with the usual (i) explainer. Two short of a heap at bedtime and the morning says so. An empty bin can be picked up and moved. Tests: 61. Fertiliser carries a grade, I to III (thin, good, rich), set by how much power the batch holds: three common crops make a thin batch, a full bin or rarer crops a rich one. The grade sits in the name and the panel, as filled dots. 62 tests.

**Build 27.** Breeding has more than one answer. The price now pays for care: a plant that needs little water or shrugs off drought sells up to 30% and 20% dearer (`genetics.valuePremium`), so a low-care line is worth breeding, not just a slow-and-big one. Rare traits (golden, glowing, regrowing, frost-hardy) appear from nothing only when the parents are from different families; same-family crosses only pass them down. That makes the cross-family gamble a distinct strategy with its own prize, and the bench says so. Every species card, in the hand, in the ground and on the bench, shows two time-cost numbers: what a bed of it earns per game day if replanted as it ripens (plants slow at night; regrowing plants count their shorter later cycles) and about how many pours a day it wants. The bench details lost the rarity number (tier and gen are enough) so they fit on one line. The compost panel's empty state tells you the first win (Speed compost on your best seller) and where stars come from. No odds wheel on the bench: the single risk number stays, the sterile / wither / throwback split stays hidden, so a cross still feels like a gamble. Tests: 65.

**Build 30.** The cart pays for what it can see. **Build 33.** Stage 3 begins: the sprinkler, the first automation. It is a catalogue item in a new Automation group ($140, each one you own makes the next 15% dearer), delivered to a pocket. Click a free tile of your land to set it down; it takes that tile. Once a game hour while you are awake it gives one pour to any bed within one tile that holds a living plant and has dried below the growing band, and never more, so it cannot waterlog (rain still can). The water is metered: $0.05 a pour at first (raised to $0.20 in Build 34), added up through the day and taken out of the night's money, shown on the morning card under the cart's sale. So a thirsty line under a sprinkler costs more to run than a low-water one, which is the thirst gene's money meaning now that the buyer no longer pays for it. Holding a sprinkler shows its reach under the cursor, pointing at a placed one shows the same, and it sprays visibly when it has just poured. The hoe picks it up. Tests: 68.

**Build 34.** Playtest fixes. The hoe now swings when it digs up, clears, or turns a bed, not only when it tills. **Build 35.** Playtest fixes around the sprinkler and fertiliser. **Build 80.** Bags you can tell apart. A bag of fertiliser is a tied sack in the colour of its strongest stat: green for Speed, gold for Quality, orange for Yield, blue for Water keep, red-brown for Hardiness, purple for Mutation, with a band per grade and a gold star per sure star. A fertilised bed shows flecks of the same colour, and gold specks for its stars, so you can read the farm without hovering.

**Build 81.** The field machines. Pickers come in three tiers that mirror the sprinklers' reach: Picker II works the 24 beds around it and takes two an hour, Picker III the 48 and three, for more power, so one picker and one sprinkler cover the same block. The planter, in the same three tiers, stands on the line and plants the empty beds in its reach with the seeds you load, working a bag of fertiliser in first when it has one; seeds or a bag in hand load it, empty hands take them back. Every machine has a switch: empty hands on a picker, hopper, dryer or planter that holds nothing turns it off and on, the dryer's panel has a button, a red dot marks an off machine, and an off hopper stops its belt. Machines never stand on beds; a bed under a picker is still yours to work by hand. 97 tests.

**Build 82.** The model farm, and bigger generators. Settings has a button that opens the model farm: a farm with every machine set up and running, a windmill on the pipe from the well, a block of carrots under a Sprinkler II with a Planter III and a Picker III on the path through it, belt to a splitter, one way to a dryer and on across the track to a hopper by the truck's crates, the other to a hopper by the porch crate. Walk it, hover everything, change things; nothing there is saved and your own farm is untouched. Generators come in three tiers: 6, 12 and 20 power for one, two and three cans a night. Belt and power line can now cross the cart track, which is how a line reaches the bay.

**Build 83.** The bay, from the playtest. The truck parks on the end of the cart track, three tiles long and drawn to scale, and when it arrives the porch crate goes: parcels come to the delivery sign from then on, and only what is on the truck's bed counts for the night. Crates are smaller, a box you lift, and nothing sets them for you: click the gravel beside the truck to set a free one, the hoe lifts an empty one away. A hopper beside a crate tips into it as the belt runs, so you can watch the crate fill. Pickers take real amounts, 4, 8 and 16 an hour by tier, and the green reach preview reads the tier of the picker in your hand or under the cursor. The planter has a panel on E like every other station, and says fertiliser, not bags. The model farm's clearing runs east to the forest road so nothing sits in the trees, its short branch ends at a large chest, and its two crates stand beside the hopper.

**Build 84.** Tiers that line up in practice. Three machines cannot share a tile, so only one of a block's sprinkler, planter and picker can stand at its centre and the other two are a tile out. A planter or picker tier is now built to work the sprinkler block of the same tier from the tile beside the sprinkler: it reaches one tile further than the sprinkler does (2, 3 and 4 for tiers I, II and III). So Sprinkler II, Planter II and Picker II on the path through a five-wide block cover all twenty beds between them, and the model farm shows exactly that.

**Build 85.** Weight. Pockets hold twelve crops a stack, not ninety-nine; the basket from the catalogue raises that to 24 and the big basket to 36; fertiliser stacks to ten; seeds are light and stack to 99 as before, and so does everything in a chest or on a counter. Pockets feel heavy from forty crops. The wheelbarrow stands by the porch chest once bought: click it to take the handles and it rolls a step ahead of you while you walk slower, click again to let go, click it with something in hand to load the stack, E opens it like a chest. Six stacks of 48. It stays home when you go to the market. Old saves spill what no longer fits a pocket into the porch chest.

**Build 86.** The forklift. Sold once the truck is here, it parks in the bay. Click it to get on: you drive at nearly twice walking pace with the forklift under you, lift a crate onto the forks with a click, three at a time, click the truck to load every full one at once, click open ground to set one down, and click the forklift or press E to get off. The market leaves it behind, as it does the barrow. Lifting by hand still works for one crate at a time.

**Build 87.** The visual pass. Produce lying in the world is drawn at its size gene, so a pea is small and a giant pumpkin fills its spot, on belts, in crates, on the stall counter and in your hands; the hotbar keeps one size so slots stay readable. A lone crop on a belt rides at three quarters of a tile and big ones glide over the next tile without being cut off. The picker, planter and generator change shape with each tier instead of wearing a colour band: a wooden picker with a wicker basket, a steel one with a claw and a crate, a two-armed one with a lamp; a seed-drill planter, wider in steel, then twin funnels; a generator with a flywheel, then a long twin-stack block. The splitter shows a fork of arrows and the filter a sieve. The bay floor has painted crate spots, crates fly from your hands or the forks onto the truck bed, and the forklift faces the way you drive, takes a moment to get going, thuds and jolts when it hits something, and hums while you are on it. Machines that need you show a lamp: amber when waiting (a planter out of seed, a dryer with nothing to dry), red blinking when blocked (a full picker, hopper or dryer tray, a full chest at a belt end, the front of a jammed belt, whose chevrons stop).

**Build 88.** Playtest fixes. The picker is a boom crane: it works whenever you are awake, one crop at a time, swinging a telescoping boom out to the ripe crop that has waited longest, snipping it with its claw and carrying it back to the basket at its real size. A trip takes 60 / perHour game minutes (about 5, 2.5 and 1.25 real seconds for tiers I to III), so the rate is the speed you watch; two pickers never reach for the same crop, and switching one off folds the boom back. The E marker is a pixel key cap on the world's grid that fades, slides between stations and bobs a pixel. Fertiliser is the original open sack again, with the stat drawn on it: flecks and a stitched label in the stat's colour, a bar for the grade and gold stars on the rim. The truck takes any crate with something in it; the messages no longer say only full ones.

**Build 89.** The tower. A picker and a planter can stand on a sprinkler's tile, stacked on its post, as long as power line runs under it: set a sprinkler on a lone picker or planter and it takes it onto its post too. All three work the same square from the same centre, so each tier now reaches exactly as far as the sprinkler of the same tier (radius 1, 2, 3) and nothing spills into the plot next door. A tower draws its machines' power together and has one switch; the hoe takes the top machine off first. It is drawn two tiles tall: the sprinkler's head on top, the picker's collar and boom high on the mast with its bin at the foot, the planter's funnel ringing it lower with a drop tube and disc. With crops in the bin, empty hands take them; seeds or fertiliser load the planter, E opens it. The model farm's block is worked by one tower.

**Build 90.** The truck's cargo box is open at the top with the tailgate down at the back, drawn in two layers so the crates stand inside it and only their tops show over the near wall. The far row fills first, from the cab back. A crate you load, by hand or off the forks, arcs to the open tailgate and slides along the floor to its place. Produce on belts is smaller and lies flat. The E key cap is smaller.

**Build 91.** The E key cap is redrawn at half-pixel detail with a margin round the E and the key's side below it, and it sits in the world like the ground does, so it moves exactly with the map as you walk; no more bob.

**Build 92.** The wheelbarrow is redrawn in three views (side, pushed toward you, pushed away), standing on its tyre and legs with its shadow under them; pushed, its legs lift and the handles come up to your hands. It sits exactly in your hands the way you face, no lag, and the farmer has a pushing pose with both hands on the handles (the tool or crop in hand is put away while pushing). The E marker no longer sits on the barrow you are pushing. "Bag is full" now says "Pockets full".

**Build 93.** Rain lives in the world: its streaks and splashes take the camera's offset like the ground does, so walking no longer drags them about. The barrow is drawn with a border so its round tyre, legs and handle ends are outlined instead of cut off, the pushed-away handles are shorter, and its wheel leaves a faint track like footprints. Produce dropped into the barrow from its panel makes a sound. A harvest is all or nothing: if it doesn't fit, it stays on the plant and the message says why (each kind and grade takes its own pocket); what your pockets can't take goes into the barrow you are pushing, and nothing is thrown away any more. The bay's gravel offers a free crate with a tool in hand too, and the truck's arrival says crates are free.

**Build 94.** Each truck tier has its own size, crates the same size in each: the Small truck is three tiles with one row of four, the Medium four tiles with a deeper box, taller cab and two rows of four, the Large five tiles with two rows of six on three axles. A bigger truck parks further down the track, nose still at the road; anything laid where it now parks goes back to the farm chest and nobody is left standing under it. The forklift faces the way you drive, up and down too: from the front the driver sits behind the mast with the crates on the forks in front, from behind you see the counterweight and the load going ahead; the E marker stays off it while you drive. The first time the truck is parked a short guide explains free crates, filling, lifting, loading and the night run, and the truck's panel has a "How the truck works" button.

**Build 95.** A crate holds 24, so the small truck carries 96 a night, twice the fully extended porch crate it replaces (Medium 192, Large 288). Set too many crates? The hoe lifts an empty one away, or carry an empty one to the truck and it is handed back. The forklift's catalogue line just says what it does.

**Build 97.** Trucks cost more again: Small 800, Medium 1500, Large 2200. Each tier still costs a little under its load ratio over the last (192 is twice 96 for under twice the price; 288 is 1.5 times 192 for 1.47 times).

**Build 96.** Truck prices grow a little slower than what they carry: the fully extended porch crate cost 285 for 48 a night, the Small truck is 540 for 96, the Medium 1000 for 192, the Large 1400 for 288. A delivery crate gets heavier as it fills: you walk at half pace with an empty one and under a third with a full one. The Medium and Large trucks need the forklift: their beds are too high to lift a crate onto by hand, and the catalogue will not sell the Medium until you have one.
